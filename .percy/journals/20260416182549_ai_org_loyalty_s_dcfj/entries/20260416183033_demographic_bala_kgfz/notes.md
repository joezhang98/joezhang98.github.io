Tested whether the Human and AI conditions are balanced on demographics at baseline, and whether any demographic variable moderates the main loyalty effect.

**Balance Checks** (all tests suggest adequate randomization):
- **Age**: Human M = 42.7 (SD = 14.7) vs. AI M = 42.5 (SD = 13.3); t(58) = 0.06, p = .956 — no difference.
- **Gender**: χ²(2) = 1.15, p = .562 — no difference.
- **Ethnicity**: χ²(4) = 3.20, p = .526 — no difference.
- **Education**: χ²(5) = 9.91, p = .078 — marginal trend only; Human condition had more \"Some college\" (33% vs. 13%) while AI condition had more \"Doctorate\" (13% vs. 0%) and \"2 year degree\" (10% vs. 0%). No correction for multiplicity applied at baseline.

**Moderation Analyses** (OLS, Condition × Moderator interaction, mean-centred moderators):
- All interaction terms were non-significant across all 6 models (all ps > .60).
- No demographic variable (Age, Gender, or Education ordinal) moderated the effect of Condition on either LOYALTY_3RD_Composite or LOYALTY_1ST_Composite.
- The main effect of Condition (Human > AI) remained significant in all models.

**Note:** Ethnicity was excluded from OLS moderation due to small cell sizes across the 5 categories.

**Caveat:** Small sample (n = 60) may be underpowered to detect moderation effects."
